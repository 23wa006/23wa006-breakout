Every frame also costs −0.002. With “Guided training” enabled, moving toward the predicted landing point earns a very small additional shaping reward. Changing reward definitions is a good extension exercise.

Prediction questions

Ask students before changing a setting:

① If curiosity starts at zero, what does the AI never discover?
② Does high patience help it pursue distant bricks?
③ Does a low training loss always mean a good game score?
④ Why could an agent learn to catch the ball but fail to clear all bricks?

Suggested classroom activity

  1. Play in Human mode for 30 seconds; identify state, actions and reward.
  2. Switch to AI mode and watch one untrained game.
  3. Train 100 episodes and compare the reward chart and paddle movement.
  4. Test with exploration turned off, then reset the network and change γ or ε.

What this demonstration actually implements

Real, browser-side Deep Q-Learning implemented with a small JavaScript neural network: online DQN, replay buffer, random minibatches, epsilon-greedy exploration, Bellman training targets, and a periodically copied target network. It is a simplified educational Breakout environment, not an Atari emulator or a pixel-based convolutional DQN. Training outcomes vary between runs.

Works offline, including AI training. Game data never leaves your browser.
